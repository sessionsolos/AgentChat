# John's hidden-money investigation

**Decision date:** 3 October 2026<br>
**Objective:** find lawful, payment-testable mechanisms that can add at least $100/day and, preferably, decouple revenue from John's hours.<br>
**Standard of proof:** a paid 48-hour test is evidence; traffic, likes, surveys, and compliments are not.

## Executive finding

John's most valuable asset is not “content expertise” in the abstract. It is a set of unusually credible **proof objects**: 104 hand-checked transcriptions, 23 original long-form interviews, 11 indexed conversation guides, named relationships with working musicians, completed guitar builds, a $995 wired product already offered for sale, and a repeatable human editing method. Session Solos publicly confirms the archive size and process; JTal publicly establishes the custom-build operation and lifetime-support positioning.[^ss-home][^ss-about][^jtal]

The investigation therefore rejects broad consulting, a generic course, ad-supported publishing, and unlicensed sale of the existing TAB archive. The best hidden money is in (a) converting proof into high-intent transactions, (b) selling permission-first derivatives to rights holders, and (c) turning bespoke judgment into fixed-input products whose production can later be delegated.

All economics below are **decision estimates, not observed sales**. “Variable cost” excludes John's labor but includes likely payment fees, parts, shipping, and contractors. Before launch, John should confirm his employment agreement, invention-assignment language, moonlighting policy, and ownership of every source file.

The archive is unusually real, but rights matter. Copyright in a musical composition is distinct from copyright in its recording, and an independently written transcription does not erase the composition owner's rights.[^copyright] Guitar Pro itself distinguishes its publisher-compensated legal catalog from unverified free tabs.[^mysongbook-legal]

## Ten survivors

### 1. Artist-authorized “official tab launch”

1. **Mechanism.** An artist, guitarist, label, or manager commissions a release-ready official guitar part: verified Guitar Pro file, PDF, a clean play-through, three vertical teaching clips, and an embeddable player/download page. The buyer supplies or warrants the necessary authority; John sells production and launch packaging, not unauthorized sheet music.
2. **Why this money exists.** Indie releases have stems and promotion budgets but rarely have a person who can both capture bends/fingering accurately and create launch media. Commodity transcription competes around $4–$15 per audio minute, leaving room above it for rights-safe verification plus distribution assets.[^paidtabs]
3. **Customer.** Self-releasing rock/pop artists, legacy session players, boutique labels, and artist managers with guitar-forward releases.
4. **Trigger.** A single/anniversary reissue is scheduled, fans are asking how to play a part, or an artist wants an official version to displace bad tabs.
5. **Offer.** “Official Part Pack”: one part up to five minutes, GP/PDF, performer approval round, play-through, three shorts, hosted landing page, and source-file handoff.
6. **Price.** $750 pilot; 50% upfront. Later: $1,250 standard, or $2,500 for three songs.
7. **Unit economics.** $750 revenue; about $45 fees/hosting; 6–8 hours initially; about $705 contribution before labor (94%).
8. **First $100.** Send a private 45-second watermarked sample for one *artist-owned original* to ten interview alumni/indie artists. Ask for a $150 nonrefundable pilot deposit against $750. One deposit clears the threshold.
9. **$100/day.** Two $750 packs/month produce about $1,410 contribution, or $47/day; four produce about $2,820, or **$94/day**. Add one $250 monthly maintenance/license renewal across ten artists ($2,500/month) and the combined mechanism exceeds $100/day.
10. **$1,000/day question.** Plausible only as a roster/platform: 20 $1,250 packs plus $5,000 of renewals per month is $30,000/month. John cannot personally transcribe that volume; trained contractors and a QA protocol are mandatory.
11. **Automation.** Intake, stem upload, rights warranty, invoice, page generation, and clip templates. Junior transcribers create drafts; John audits only difficult passages.
12. **Existing advantage.** The public archive proves both accuracy and neglected-session-player specialization.[^ss-about]
13. **Competitive defense.** A growing correction corpus, trusted artist approvals, niche relationships, and simultaneous notation/video competence.
14. **Fatal flaw.** Artists may like the artifact but have no budget or measurable return; transcription hours can balloon.
15. **Legal/rights check.** Require written authorization covering notation, audio excerpts, video, names, likenesses, hosting term, territories, and downstream use. Do not sell existing third-party compositions until a publisher license is executed.
16. **48-hour test (≤$100).** Build one sample from John's own music or a prospect's expressly supplied original; use a Stripe payment link and ten individualized emails. Spend $0–$30. Validation is one paid $150 deposit.
17. **Kill condition.** Stop outbound after 30 qualified, opened pitches yield zero deposits or fewer than three calls; do not “solve” that with a course.

### 2. Fan-funded, permission-first tab bounty

1. **Mechanism.** A fan, fan club, or guitar community funds an accuracy bounty for a requested song, but work starts only after the artist/publisher authorizes publication. Funds are authorized or held by a compliant commerce/crowdfunding provider, not casually escrowed by John. The finished tab remains free; John is paid for commissioned research and production.
2. **Why this money exists.** Demand for obscure parts is fragmented across fans, while rights clearance and accurate production have fixed costs. Aggregating several fans reveals willingness to pay without putting the composition behind John's paywall.
3. **Customer.** The cleanest first buyer is the artist or fan-club operator; later, multiple fans can sponsor through a platform that supports conditional campaigns.
4. **Trigger.** A request reaches a published threshold and the rights holder approves.
5. **Offer.** $400 “authorized bounty”: one verified part, approval round, free public page, and sponsor acknowledgments with no editorial control.
6. **Price.** $400/song; suggested four sponsor slots at $100, or artist-funded $400.
7. **Unit economics.** $400 revenue; about $20 fees; 4–6 hours; about $380 contribution (95%).
8. **First $100.** Ask one artist already interviewed to authorize an original/controlled song and invite one identified superfan or fan club to buy the first $100 sponsor slot, with a refund if the $400 threshold is missed.
9. **$100/day.** Eight completed bounties/month × $380 = $3,040/month, about **$101/day**.
10. **$1,000/day question.** Not as handcrafted transcription. It could feed a much larger authorized catalog, but then licensing, contributor QA, and software—not John—must carry output.
11. **Automation.** Voting, thresholds, refunds, permission records, status emails, templates, and contributor assignment.
12. **Existing advantage.** Session Solos already has the exact archive, search interface, and fan promise needed to make a bounty believable.[^ss-home]
13. **Competitive defense.** Rights-holder relationships plus a reputation for correcting niche material.
14. **Fatal flaw.** Clearance friction can make every campaign stall before revenue.
15. **Legal/rights check.** Written print/digital/arrangement permission is gating, not cleanup. Disclose sponsorship, refund rules, delivery timing, and data use. Do not call funds “escrow” unless a qualified provider actually supplies it.
16. **48-hour test (≤$100).** Test exactly one artist-controlled original with written permission and a $100 checkout. Email known fans directly; do not count votes.
17. **Kill condition.** Kill the fan-funded format if an authorized title shown to 100 high-intent page visitors produces no payment, or if the first three permissions each take more than two hours to secure.

### Validation sequence

1. Create **three separate payment links**, each with scope, refund rules, delivery date, and capacity of one or two—not waitlists.
2. Use only owned/public/non-employer prospect lists. No mass ads and no build-out.
3. Run 30 qualified direct offers per test or until capacity sells.
4. Record opens/replies only diagnostically. The decision variable is **paid orders/deposits**.
5. Deliver the first order manually and measure total minutes, refunds, objections, and follow-on demand before automating.

## What all 100 agents could have missed

### 1. Post-purchase evidence is a separate market from pre-purchase advice

The initial instinct is to help choose a guitar. The more defensible moment may be the arrival window, when an expensive transaction has already happened, the customer is highly motivated, and neutral documentation has option value. This is why opportunity 10 survives separately from opportunity 5.

### 2. The interview archive may be more valuable as a rights-cleared *index* than as media

Even if releases block clip licensing, John's timestamps and topic graph can still route producers to primary sources, drive paid research, and identify whom to re-contact for a new licensed interview. A failed footage audit need not destroy opportunity 3.

### 3. The free TAB archive can be an enterprise QA benchmark without distributing compositions

With counsel and publisher permission where necessary, John could license a carefully scoped evaluation service to music-AI companies: they submit model output for music they control; John scores technique, fingering/playability, rhythm, and articulation against an authorized reference and returns aggregate error analysis. Recent research still identifies expressive techniques and string/fret assignment as hard problems, supporting the need for human evaluation.[^amt-research] This was not promoted into the ten because buyer access and data/rights boundaries are highly uncertain. A lawful test is one $500 paid benchmark on customer-owned stems; kill it after 20 relevant company pitches with no paid pilot.

## Immediate rights and conflict gate

Before taking money, John should create a one-page ledger for each opportunity:

- **Owner:** who owns the underlying composition, recording, footage, transcript, data, design, and deliverable?
- **Permission:** what written license/release authorizes the exact media, term, territory, and commercial use?
- **Employment:** was any asset made on employer time/equipment, derived from employer confidential information, or aimed at restricted customers?
- **Source terms:** does each marketplace, website, or API permit the collection and commercial use proposed?
- **Claims:** what is observation versus inference, and what result is explicitly not guaranteed?
- **Privacy/security:** what is collected, where is it stored, who can access it, and when is it deleted?
- **Insurance/tax:** do product, errors-and-omissions, sales-tax, or local business rules apply?

This report is commercial research, not legal advice. For TAB licensing, interview releases, employment conflicts, data collection, and product liability, obtain advice from qualified counsel before scaling.
